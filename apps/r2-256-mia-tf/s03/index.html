Why Hearth

Everything an assistant knows,
stays at home.

Hearth runs open-weight language, vision and speech models on hardware built for the living room — not the data center.

Air-gapped by default

No account, no cloud key, no telemetry. Hearth ships with its radios ready but idle — the moment you want, pull the physical disconnect and it answers from memory alone.

Knows your house

Point it at recipes, invoices, baby monitors or the garage manual. Hearth indexes your files on-device and answers with your context — not a stranger's internet.

Quiet, in every sense

A fanless 18 W design sips less power than a router and hums less than a library. Leave it on the shelf; you'll hear the kettle first.

Every screen, one brain

Phones, laptops and the TV talk to Hearth over your Wi-Fi. One private brain, every room — with per-room memory you can wipe in a tap.

Open models, yours to swap

Choose from a curated shelf of open-weight models, or sideload your own fine-tune. No subscription gates the best one.

Fail-closed privacy

If a request ever tries to leave, a hardware watchdog blocks it and lights the ember ring red. You always know when something is outbound. (It won't be.)

No. Inference, file indexing and speech recognition all run on the device. Hearth has no cloud service to talk to — the only network traffic is your own devices reaching it over your LAN. If you enable an optional weather or news skill, Hearth shows you exactly what will be sent before it sends anything, and the ember ring glows while it does.

Hearth One runs open models up to 34 billion parameters, quantized to fit its 16 GB of unified memory. For frontier-level reasoning you'd still reach for a workstation — or Hearth Studio with 64 GB.

Nothing changes. Hearth never needed the internet in the first place. Chat, voice, document search and home control keep working from local memory. Skills that genuinely require the outside world — like package tracking — pause politely and pick up when you're back online.

Yes. HearthOS ships with a curated shelf of open-weight models you can install in two taps, and Studio unlocks full sideloading: drop in any GGUF or safetensors file that fits in memory, set it as default, or route specific rooms to specific models. Your fine-tunes stay on your device.

The aluminum shell is fanless and passively cooled; under normal chat loads it draws about 18 W — roughly a third of a typical router setup — and peaks at 65 W during long reasoning runs. There are no moving parts, so there is nothing to hum, click or spin. Measured noise: 0 dB.
