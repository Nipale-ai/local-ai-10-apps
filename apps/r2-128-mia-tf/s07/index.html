How a language model writes, one word at a time

A language model does not write whole sentences. It looks at the text so far, gives every possible next word a probability, picks one, and repeats. Step through a real example and watch the odds.

Temperature reshapes the odds: low = play it safe, high = wild and creative.

For experts

p(word) = e^(s ÷ T) ÷ Σ e^(s ÷ T)

Each candidate carries a raw score (a logit). Dividing the logits by the temperature T and running softmax turns them into probabilities that add up to 100 %. Small T sharpens the peak, large T flattens it.

We also cut the list to the five best guesses (that is top-k, k = 5) before sampling, so nonsense words never get a chance.
